Digital Marketing Plan

Target-Audience Profile

Corporate CSR decision-makers

Profile: South African business owners, CSI managers and HR leaders, typically 30–55, with authority over community investment budgets.

Motivation: measurable education impact, credible partnerships and visible employee engagement.

Needs and objections: clear costs, reporting, compliance evidence and confidence that funds reach learners.

Best channels: LinkedIn, email case studies, search ads and partnership meetings.

Customer Persona

Nomsa Mbeki — The impact-focused CSR manager

Background: Nomsa is 38, works for a medium-sized South African company and manages an annual community-investment budget. She uses LinkedIn, Google Search and email to research credible education partnerships.

Goals: fund a visible programme, demonstrate measurable learner impact and create a meaningful employee-engagement opportunity.

Pain points: unclear use of funds, complicated sponsorship forms, weak reporting and uncertainty about whether a project is legitimate.

Decision triggers: transparent route costs, evidence such as attendance and learner numbers, a clear contact person and a simple next step.

Message and CTA: “Sponsor a route and receive clear evidence of the learners and communities your organisation helps.” Discuss a partnership.

Individual donors and families

Profile: Adults aged 25–65 who care about education and want a simple, trustworthy way to give monthly.

Motivation: helping a learner directly and seeing a tangible local outcome.

Needs and objections: affordable options, secure contact details, proof of impact and an easy signup.

Best channels: Facebook, Instagram, email stories and community referrals.

Volunteers and advocates

Profile: Students, educators, community members and professionals willing to share, volunteer or introduce partners.

Motivation: practical participation and improving access to education in the Eastern Cape.

Needs and objections: clear time commitments, safe participation and specific actions they can take.

Best channels: Instagram, Facebook, community groups, email and referral links.

Campaign analysis

Sample results show 4,820 visitors, 318 subscribers, a 42% email open rate, 96 CTA clicks and 18 sponsorship applications.

The 42% open rate is above the 40% KPI, but the 2.0% CTA click rate is below the 2.5% target. Applications have reached 60% of the target of 30.

Interpretation: the message earns attention, but the page and CTA need to convert more interested visitors into action.

Illustrative email funnel: using a separate sample baseline of 1,240 delivered emails, a 38% open rate implies about 471 opens, a 6.4% click-through rate implies about 79 clicks, and a 2.1% conversion rate implies about 26 applications. These rounded figures are sample data, not observed or verified campaign results.

Decision: preserve the current message as the control, test segment-specific subject lines and landing-page copy, simplify sponsorship form completion, and wait for an adequate sample size before declaring a winner.

Optimisation recommendations

  1. Test the action-led headline and shorter sponsor CTA against the impact-led variant.
  2. Move proof points and the sponsorship CTA above the fold on mobile.
  3. Send follow-up emails to people who opened but did not click.
  4. Reduce sponsorship-form fields and measure completion rate by segment.
  5. Review CTA rate, applications and unsubscribe rate weekly after deployment.

Technical interview preparation

  • Why React? To compose reusable email, routing, dashboard and form components.
  • How does routing work? Hash changes update React state and select the journey view.
  • How is data stored? Demo leads are JSON records in browser localStorage with a production API as the next step.
  • How was quality checked? Manual route/form tests, responsive checks, platform diagnostics and KPI review.
  • What is the key limitation? CDN React and localStorage are suitable for demonstration, not secure production data processing.

One-page optimisation recommendation

Decision: prioritise CTA conversion before increasing campaign reach.

Evidence: the sample campaign records a 42% email open rate, above the 40% target, but a 2.0% CTA click rate, below the 2.5% target, and 18 of 30 sponsorship applications.

Actions for the next 30 days:

  1. Run the action-led headline, “Sponsor a route. Open the way to school,” against the impact-led headline.
  2. Place one primary sponsor CTA above the fold and reduce the sponsorship form to essential fields.
  3. Show route cost, learner reach and proof of impact beside the CTA.

Review method: compare both headline variants over the same two-week traffic period, then review dashboard results weekly by audience segment.

Individual learning reflection

This project strengthened my ability to connect front-end development with campaign planning. I learned that a working website is not enough: each page section and CTA must guide a specific audience toward a measurable action.

I improved my understanding of reusable React components, state management, routing, browser data storage, validation and deployment through GitHub Actions. Building the dashboard also showed me how sample data can support decisions when live advertising and analytics platforms are unavailable, provided that assumptions are clearly labelled.

The main challenge was integrating a large single-file HTML prototype with React and deployment requirements. I addressed it by isolating features into mounted components, documenting the limitations of CDN React and localStorage, and adding a platform check. In future, I would separate the application into a standard React build, connect a secure API/CRM, add automated tests and use real consent-based analytics before handling production leads.
